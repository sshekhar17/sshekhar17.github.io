STEP 2

Transform the prototype

Choose Φ(ω); the normalized prototype and gain G do the rest.

LPF · frequency scaling

Φ(ω) = ω/ωc
Free parameter: ωc > 0.
Move the normalized prototype cutoff from 1 to ω = ωc.

Candidate filter

|HN^(jω)| = G 1+|Φ(ω)|2N
(i) Choose Φ; (ii) evaluate the Butterworth prototype HN at Φ(ω); (iii) multiply by G. G is the transformed peak-passband gain, not always its DC gain.
Free parameters: G > 0, integer N ≥ 1, ωc > 0.

Design challenge

Can you find the smallest possible integer N without looking at the answer? Try changing N, G, and the cutoff edge(s) directly.

The mask is satisfied only when both conditions read PASS.

Minimum-order Butterworth design

What should you notice?

  • Order: increasing N sharpens the transition but increases circuit complexity.
  • Frequency parameters: moving ωc (or ω1, ω2) helps one mask boundary but can hurt another.
  • Gain: the gain G is a free parameter. Try unlocking G and moving it away from G2.
  • Band filters: for minimum order, the geometric center of the relevant mask edges is useful, but ω1, ω2 are the actual free parameters.
  • Minimum N: the first order with a nonempty feasible cutoff interval wins.
  • Band filters: a prototype of order N transforms into a physical filter of order 2N.
