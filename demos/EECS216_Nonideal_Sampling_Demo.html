1. Two ways to form finite-width samples

Time domain

A practical sampler produces pulses with nonzero duration. In flat-top sampling, each pulse holds the value measured at its starting time. In natural sampling, the output follows the original signal whenever the rectangular gate is open.

Show the mathematical expressions
pτ(t) = 1 for 0 ≤ t < τ, and 0 otherwise
xFT(t) = ∑n∈ℤ x(nTs)pτ(t − nTs)
xN(t) = x(t) ∑n∈ℤ pτ(t − nTs)

Natural sampling is also called switching: it retains the behavior of x(t) throughout each open window, not just its values at isolated sample instants.

Flat-top sampling holds a number; natural sampling opens a gate for the original waveform.

2. What happens to the spectral replicas?

Frequency domain

Both sampling methods produce frequency-shifted copies of the original spectrum. However, flat-top sampling multiplies the entire spectrum by a sinc-shaped envelope, whereas natural sampling scales each replica by a constant.

YOUR TASK · Change τ, then compare the two spectra. In the full-spectrum view, look at the shifted copies; in the baseband view, compare their shapes.

Spectra are normalized by the duty cycle D so their shapes can be compared on a common scale.

Flat-top sampling · |XFT(jω)|/D

Orange spectrum; dashed curve is the magnitude of the sinc envelope

Natural sampling · |XN(jω)|/D

Green spectrum; each replica has its own constant amplitude factor

Show the frequency-domain derivation

For the rectangular pulse pτ(t),

Pτ(jω) = τ sinc(ωτ/(2π)) e−jωτ/2
XFT(jω) = [Pτ(jω)/Ts] ∑k∈ℤ X(j(ω − kωs))
XN(jω) = (1/Ts) ∑k∈ℤ Pτ(jkωs) X(j(ω − kωs))

We use sinc(u)=sin(πu)/(πu). In flat-top sampling the envelope varies with ω across each replica. In natural sampling, Pτ(jkωs) depends only on the replica index k.

The central copy of the natural-sampling spectrum is D·X(jω), but flat-top sampling also attenuates its higher baseband frequencies.

3. Can we recover the original signal?

Reconstruction

Pass each sampled signal through an ideal low-pass filter that retains only the original baseband, then compensate for the duty-cycle gain. Natural sampling recovers x(t) directly; flat-top sampling generally retains frequency-dependent attenuation.

Show why the reconstruction methods differ

Within |ω| ≤ ωB, after the ideal LPF the natural-sampling output equals D·X(jω). Dividing by D therefore restores X(jω).

Natural: XN,LPF(jω) = D X(jω)

For flat-top sampling, the LPF output contains an additional frequency-dependent factor:

Flat-top: XFT,LPF(jω) = D e−jωτ/2 sinc(ωτ/(2π)) X(jω)

After gain correction and removal of the known delay τ/2, the remaining attenuation is sinc(ωτ/(2π)). The equalizer compensates for this factor wherever it is nonzero. In our chosen parameter range it has no zeros in the source passband.

Natural sampling: LPF + gain. Flat-top sampling: LPF + gain, and generally an equalizer to correct the pulse-response distortion.
